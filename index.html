Energy and heat

First Law of Thermodynamics

ΔU = Q − W

Energy is conserved. Heat Q added to a system either raises its internal energy U or leaves as work W done by the gas. Sign convention here: Q in is +, W done by the gas is +.

Sensible heat

Q = m · c · ΔT

Heat that changes temperature without changing phase. c is specific heat (J/g·K): ice 2.09, water 4.186, steam 2.01.

Latent heat

Q = m · L

Heat absorbed or released during a phase change at constant temperature. Lf = 334 J/g (melting), Lv = 2260 J/g (boiling). The energy breaks molecular bonds instead of speeding molecules up.

Conduction

Q/t = k·A·ΔT / L

Heat flow through a solid. k is thermal conductivity, A area, L thickness. Metals have large k.

Convection

Q/t = h·A·ΔT

Heat carried by a moving fluid. h is the convection coefficient. Hot fluid rises, cool fluid sinks, forming currents.

Radiation

Q/t = ε·σ·A·T⁴

Electromagnetic emission, needing no medium. σ = 5.67×10⁻⁸ W/m²K⁴, ε is emissivity, T in kelvin.

Gas laws and kinetic theory

Ideal gas law

PV = nRT

R = 8.314 J/mol·K. With P in kPa and V in litres, PV comes out in joules. T must be in kelvin: K = °C + 273.15.

Boyle’s law

P₁V₁ = P₂V₂

At constant T and n, squeezing a gas into half the volume doubles the pressure.

Charles’s law

V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂

At constant P and n, volume is proportional to absolute temperature. This is what the piston in module 4 does.

Gay-Lussac’s law

P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂

At constant V and n, pressure rises in proportion to absolute temperature.

Kinetic energy of a molecule

KEavg = (3/2)·kB·T

Temperature is a measure of the average translational kinetic energy. kB = 1.38×10⁻²³ J/K.

Root-mean-square speed

vrms = √(3RT / M)

M is molar mass in kg/mol. Lighter gases such as helium move faster than heavier ones at the same temperature.

Pressure from collisions

P = (1/3)(N/V)·m·v²rms

Pressure is the average force of countless molecular impacts on the walls. It links the microscopic motion to PV = nRT.

Internal energy

U = (3/2)·n·R·T

For a monatomic ideal gas, U depends only on temperature. Hence ΔU = 0 in any isothermal process.

Molar heat capacities

Cv = 3R/2 · Cp = 5R/2 · Cp − Cv = R

Cp is larger because heat at constant pressure must also pay for expansion work. γ = Cp/Cv = 5/3.

Thermodynamic processes

Isochoric (V constant)

W = 0, Q = ΔU = nCvΔT

No volume change means no work, so all heat raises internal energy.

Isobaric (P constant)

W = PΔV = nRΔT, Q = nCpΔT

Heat splits into ΔU (60 %) and W (40 %) for a monatomic gas.

Isothermal (T constant)

ΔU = 0, Q = W = nRT·ln(V₂/V₁)

The gas stays at one temperature, so all absorbed heat becomes work.

Adiabatic (Q = 0)

T₂ = T₁(V₁/V₂)γ−1, W = −ΔU

With no heat exchange, expansion work comes from internal energy, so the gas cools. Compression heats it.

Worked example

Problem: How much heat turns 100 g of ice at −20 °C into steam at 100 °C?

Q₁ = 100 × 2.09 × 20 = 4,180 J (warm ice)
Q₂ = 100 × 334 = 33,400 J (melt)
Q₃ = 100 × 4.186 × 100 = 41,860 J (warm water)
Q₄ = 100 × 2260 = 226,000 J (boil)
Qtotal ≈ 305.4 kJ

Latent heat (Q₂ + Q₄) is about 85 % of the total. Check this in module 1 by setting the mass to 100 g.
